## 1. Fonollosa’s minimum cyclic modulus

**Canonical target.** Fonollosa (2026), Conjecture 1. For integers $n\ge2$ and $1\le N<2^n-2^{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}$, every $n$-element set $A\subseteq\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ admits a multiplicity vector $(m_a)_{a\in A}\in\mathbb N^A$ other than the all-ones vector with $\sum_a m_a=n$ and $\sum_a m_a a=\sum_a a$ in the cyclic group. In plain terms, below that modulus you can always replace the original list by one with repeats and omissions that has the same length and modular sum. A proof would pin the shortest cyclic transform in the paper’s exact permanent extraction scheme. [Original paper, Conjecture 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08366v2) · [Permanent application](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09949)

**Status and prior work.** The universal claim remains open in the sources checked November 22. Fonollosa reports Lean verification for the set $\{2^k-1:0\le k<n\}$ and computational exclusions through $n=7$. Inal obtains $N\ge2^{n-1}$ and classifies equality over all finite abelian groups. Sitjar’s November 14 preprint improves the cyclic lower bound to $2^{n-1}+\max(2n-2,\lceil(2^{n-1}-1)/(n-1)\rceil)$ for $n\ge5$. It also reports exhaustive verification for $n=8,9$ using two programs that share a normalization and insertion test. The board inspected the paper and did not replay the computations. [Fixed-set Lean development](https://github.com/jarfo/min-modulus/tree/e7c78dd63955092b5f8d8a5fa826476337c0f4be) · [Inal](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/C58Q9) · [Sitjar, Theorems A and B](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24228247)

**Route and obstacles.** Study whether the restrictions on translated subset sums and their shifted copies force a larger excluded region in cyclic groups. Sitjar’s bound still has leading term $2^{n-1}$, and Fonollosa’s sharp digit-sum argument depends on binary weights. One valid set below the threshold suffices to refute the conjecture. Novelty risk is substantial because Sitjar already settled two small cases computationally and Fonollosa reports nonexhaustive searches through $n=13$.

**Lean and first experiment.** The pinned `Arxiv.«2607.08366».min_modulus` matches the source quantifiers and guards positive moduli. `Finset`, `ZMod`, `Nat.log` and the collision lemma give a short route to checking a candidate counterexample. In one CPU workday, cross-check a multiplicity-vector checker and size/residue dynamic program at $n=8, N=248$ and replay Sitjar’s prime case $N=241$ as a negative control. Then pilot a fixed-time collision-clause search at $n=10, N=1009$, retaining the search trace and using prime-modulus affine normalization. Extend only if the pilot yields a witness, a replayable complete exclusion, or pruning that points to a general theorem. [Pinned Lean source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/2607.08366/MinModulus.lean#L38-L97)

The October 22 public registration explicitly names Conjecture 1 and supplies no new theorem. Check its follow-ups and Sitjar’s exact coverage before testing new slices. [Public registration](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/20156)

**Board assessment.** High-value. 84/100. Grades in model order (4, 4, 5, 5, 3, 4) out of five. One exact witness can resolve the original question. Current lower bounds and known positive controls constrain the search. Uncertainty: Moderate.

## 2. Balister–Győri–Schelp prescribed differences

**Statement.** For every integer $s\ge2$, let $v_1,\ldots,v_{2^{s-1}}$ be an indexed list of nonzero vectors in $\mathbb F_2^s$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$. There is a partition of $\mathbb F_2^s$ into pairs $\{x_i,y_i\}$ indexed by the list with $x_i-y_i=v_i$ for every $i$. In plain terms, each vector requests a gap between two points, and every point must be used exactly once. Repeated requests count separately. [Original Conjecture 1, author manuscript](https://www.memphis.edu/msci/people/pbalistr/setcolorf.pdf) · [Published paper, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2010.11.008).

**Frontier and significance.** Open in general in the latest exact-scope sources located through November 22, 2026. The problem connects prescribed matchings, additive combinatorics and functional batch codes. The original authors report verification through dimension five. Kovács proves the conjecture when there are at most $s-2\log_2 s-1$ distinct differences, and asymptotically when at least a $1/2+\epsilon$ fraction are equal. A July 2026 preprint reports an affirmative proof for the two-internal-difference case relative to a hyperplane and symmetric even-multiplicity families with four internal differences. The classical binary Hall case has all differences crossing the hyperplane. The recent partial work raises priority risk for nearby subcases. No full proof claim was located. [Kovács, revised September 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17433) · [Zabokritskiy, July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08630).

**Lean.** `Arxiv.«2607.08630».balister_gyori_schelp_conjecture` uses an equivalence from indexed pairs of endpoints into the ambient space, enforcing distinct endpoints and exhaustive coverage. Addition equals subtraction in characteristic two. It retains all multiplicities and the exact zero-sum requirement. Mathlib's finite types, equivalences and finite field arithmetic give a direct route to checking explicit matchings. Character methods and the unbounded combinatorial reduction would require formal development. [Pinned declaration](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/2607.08630/BalisterGyoriSchelp.lean#L45).

**Research plan.** The obstacle is moving beyond a few internal differences without losing the boundary data of the remaining matching. Compare character-sum noncancellation with constructive pairing rules. A disproof requires an exact infeasibility certificate for one valid list. Start by encoding selected zero-XOR multisets in dimensions 4–6 as pair-cover problems with exact per-difference counts. Use small dimensions and published crossing and two-internal cases as controls, then investigate dimension-six profiles with four internal differences outside the symmetric family. Keep integer endpoints and inspect how the unmatched boundary varies. Initial selected instances fit a CPU pilot. Exhaustive multiset enumeration and small-dimensional success can scale poorly and provide no general proof.

**Board assessment.** High-value. 84/100. Grades in model order (5, 3, 5, 5, 3, 4) out of five. A major combinatorial target with a clean exact-cover model. Either direction needs harder certificates as dimensions grow. Uncertainty: Moderate.

## 3. Unfair 0–1 polynomial factorization

**Canonical ID and exact statement.** MathOverflow 339137, also Green Problem 28. For monic $P,Q\in\mathbb R[X]$ with nonnegative coefficients, if every coefficient of $PQ$ belongs to $\{0,1\}$, then every coefficient of both factors belongs to $\{0,1\}$. Equivalently, if two independent, finitely supported integer-valued random variables have a sum uniform on its support, each is uniform on its own support. A resolution would clarify when an exact convolution can conceal biases in its components. [Original question, Aug. 25, 2019](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/339137) · [Green Problem 28](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf#problem.28)

**Status and partial results.** Full question remained open in the latest located sources during the Nov. 22, 2026 check. The original discussion reports exact Gröbner checks through product degree 32 and links an AI-assisted Lean artifact for palindromic products. Ghidelli uses forced recurrences for restricted trinomials. Hasanbegović’s Nov. 10 preprint gives sufficient separation conditions for binary products with two blocks. Baravelli’s Nov. 20 Hexagon v2 gives an AI-generated proof claim for arbitrary trinomial factors, with a linked formalization and no human-understanding attestation. The present review did not replay these artifacts. Recent progress makes duplication risk high. [Ghidelli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09843) · [Hasanbegović](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14596) · [Baravelli v2](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00674v2) · [Palomar entry and Lean declarations](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-20-000022&version=1) · [Palindromic Lean artifact](https://github.com/TheSil/palindromic_zero_one_polynomials)

**Strategy and first experiment.** Overlapping coefficient contributions can hide fractional values. Determine how frequently one can nevertheless recover the second factor by a forced recurrence. After a baseline check with small known fair products, enumerate normalized four-term supports $0<r<s<m\le12$ with two unknown interior coefficients. For bounded product degree, use exact polynomial constraints with the recurrence and keep candidate systems as regression fixtures. A surviving algebraic candidate warrants exact verification, while systematic infeasibility may suggest an induction on overlap patterns. Finite or floating-point screens alone settle neither algebraic feasibility nor the global conjecture. Allow several CPU days for the initial classifier and revisit growth before raising degree.

**Lean.** `Mathoverflow339137.mathoverflow_339137` directly encodes the polynomial problem with `Polynomial.coeffs`, the nonzero coefficients. `Green28.green_28` is the probability formulation. The declared bridge should be checked separately. Mathlib polynomial coefficients, monicity, support and real arithmetic support the main formulation. A rational witness is light to check, while spectral estimates or exact algebraic certificates add burden. [Pinned Lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Mathoverflow/339137.lean)

**Board assessment.** High-value. 81/100. Grades in model order (4, 4, 4, 5, 3, 4) out of five. Coefficient recurrences and exact algebra give two routes. General overlaps and current competing partials lower confidence. Uncertainty: Moderate.

## 4. Fanelli–Moscardelli approximate roommates

**Statement.** For $n$ agents with complete strict preferences, put being single in last place. Let $K(n)$ be the smallest positive integer $k$ such that every profile admits a possibly partial matching with no unmatched pair of agents who both improve their partner rank by at least $k$ positions by pairing together. The Lean target asserts that some $n_0\ge3$ satisfies $K(n)=\lceil n/2\rceil$ for all $n\ge n_0$. This would give the exact worst-case tolerance required to guarantee a stable outcome. [Fanelli and Moscardelli, ECAI 2025, published online August 25, 2026, Section 5](https://doi.org/10.3233/FAIA251234).

**Frontier.** No exact resolution was located through November 22, 2026. The authors prove a universal upper bound $\lceil n/2\rceil$ and exhibit arbitrarily large odd and even markets requiring thresholds of order $\sqrt n$. They report exhaustive experiments through six agents. Theorem 2 shows that dynamics can cycle one step below the upper bound, even when fair. Existence of a stable matching is a separate property. Section 5 suggests that the bound is tight for larger sizes. The eventual equality is DeepMind’s precise interpretation of that informal suggestion. [Paper, Theorems 1–3 and Section 5](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3233/FAIA251234).

**Lean.** `FanelliMoscardelli.stability_threshold_tight` uses `Fin n` preferences as permutations with self last and matches as involutions with singleton fixed points. The exact rank inequality is additive and the acceptability graph is complete. Existing `Roommates` definitions, graph, permutation and finite arithmetic APIs support the core model. Formalizing the published upper bound and a scalable extremal construction would still be necessary for a complete proof. [Pinned declaration](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Paper/ApproximatelyStableMatching.lean#L41).

**Research plan.** To reach equality, construct for each sufficiently large even and odd $n$ a profile in which every matching has a blocking pair at $k=\lceil n/2\rceil-1$. A substantially better universal upper bound on infinitely many sizes would disprove eventual equality. First reproduce the small-size existence tests with an independent enumerator of all partial matchings. Then use rank constraints and an exact matching oracle to search for bad profiles at $n=7,8$, looking for a repeatable pattern across both parities. A bounded CPU pilot is viable. Enumerating every profile directly scales poorly. One anomalous small size cannot refute eventual equality, and a cycling example cannot establish nonexistence.

**Board assessment.** Quick-win route. 80/100. Grades in model order (3, 4, 4, 5, 4, 5) out of five. Known upper bound and a constructive lower-bound goal give a cheap first decision. Need an all-size construction. Uncertainty: Moderate; eventual scope interpreted.

## 5. Finite magma implication E677 → E255

**Canonical ID and exact statement.** Equational Theories Project Problem 8.1, `EquationalTheories_677_255.Finite.Equation677_implies_Equation255`. Does every finite set with a binary operation $\diamond$ satisfying $x=y\diamond(x\diamond((y\diamond x)\diamond y))$ for every $x,y$ also satisfy $x=((x\diamond x)\diamond x)\diamond x$ for every $x$? This is the last unresolved implication, up to duality, reported after the project considered 22,028,942 finite implications. The infinite counterexample falls outside this target. [Project paper, Dec. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07087) · [Tao’s account](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2025/12/09/the-equational-theories-project-advancing-collaborative-mathematical-research-at-scale/)

**Status and partial results.** The project blueprint proves that left translations in a finite E677 magma are permutations. It also shows E255 is equivalent to every point $x$ having some left fixer $y\diamond x=x$. As of Nov. 22, 2026, targeted searches found no full finite resolution. A July 22 issue reports a Lean orbit lemma and all 45 order-10 SAT cases refuted by DRAT, subject to review of encoding and symmetry coverage. The claimed archive is 2.8 GB and was not replayed here. An October 14 submission proves a conditional row-transport result. An independent public project studies period-three residual configurations, so broad undirected enumeration would duplicate active work. [Blueprint](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/677-chapter.html) · [Order-10 issue](https://github.com/teorth/equational_theories/issues/1464) · [Conditional result](https://prove2.me/theorems/daa82c3e-33eb-4ba3-8e17-84ad0b44e823) · [Competing investigation](https://github.com/Grisha-Pochuev/finite-magma-e677-to-e255)

**Strategy and first experiment.** Translate the fixer question into constraints on the orbit of a point in the image of right multiplication. Independently reproduce one tractable published order-10 orbit case with an audited finite-table checker. Then ask whether an identical exclusion certificate can be derived from a bounded local identity independent of cardinality. A finite table violating E255 would resolve the whole question and be cheap to check, whereas a further bounded exclusion still needs a global bridge. Time-box the baseline and structural probe to several CPU days. Scaling the existing SAT proof archives is the resource risk.

**Lean.** The pinned theorem quantifies over finite magmas and matches both equational laws. Mathlib finite types, function permutations and the Equational Theories project’s existing API make a countermodel easy to formalize. A positive universal result requires new structural mathematics. [Pinned Lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Other/EquationalTheories_677_255.lean)

**Board assessment.** High-value. 77/100. Grades in model order (4, 3, 5, 5, 2, 4) out of five. Unique project gap and cheap countermodel validation. Search coverage and strong competition constrain the first move. Uncertainty: Moderate; bounded claims unreplayed.

## 6. Weak versus proper tiling by intervals

**Canonical ID and exact statement.** Kolountzakis–Lev–Matolcsi, Problem 4.2, `WeakTiling.problem_4_2`. Let Ω be the union of at least three nonempty bounded open intervals on the real line with strictly separated endpoints. If a positive locally finite Borel measure ν satisfies $1_Ω*ν=1_{Ω^c}$ Lebesgue almost everywhere, does some such measure have the form $\sum_{t\in T}\delta_t$? In other words, can fractional translates that exactly cover the complement always be replaced by a proper tiling? A positive answer would establish the spectral-implies-tile direction of Fuglede’s conjecture for finite unions of intervals. [Original question, revised Sept. 16, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23631v2#S4.SS2)

**Status and partial results.** The original paper proves that weak tiling measures are pure point and constrains their atoms and gap lengths to semigroups generated by interval lengths. For commensurable lengths, Theorem 3.8 places all shifts on a lattice after scaling. The paper also handles two intervals of unequal length. As of Nov. 22, 2026, searches for Problem 4.2 and its exact wording found no resolution. Kitamura’s AI-assisted Lean artifact addresses Problem 4.3, the stronger assertion that every weak tiling measure decomposes into proper ones. Its example admits a proper tiling. This makes it a valuable control for the proposed experiment and a source of direct competition. [Paper, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23631v2#S3) · [Kitamura artifact](https://github.com/KitaKen1/weak-tiling-counterexample)

**Strategy and first experiment.** The bottleneck is proving that a genuinely fractional tile shape has no proper tiling, or explaining why any fractional coverage can be rounded. Reproduce Kitamura’s integer stencil as a control, then enumerate stencils with 3–5 runs and diameter at most 24. Look for nonnegative rational weights on trial periods up to 80 with unit weight at the origin and exact coverage on every residue. For promising witnesses, use the integer lattice reduction and a binary finite-state recurrence to test for any bi-infinite unit-weight tiling. A complete obstruction must cover every state, every period and the allowed real translations. The first CPU pilot fits a few days if run in increasing diameter with a fixed time cap. Large transition graphs and missing lattice reduction are the main failure modes.

**Lean.** The pinned declaration faithfully captures the almost-everywhere complement equality and strictly separated intervals. Mathlib measure and indicator APIs and the adjacent LRAT example support a certificate route. Formalizing the full real-to-integer bridge could dominate the effort even after finding a finite witness. [Pinned Lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Paper/WeakTiling.lean)

**Board assessment.** High-value. 76/100. Grades in model order (5, 3, 3, 5, 3, 3) out of five. Original path to a significant tiling result. A full negative certificate needs the translation and infinite-coverage bridges. Uncertainty: Moderate; real-to-discrete bridge.

## 7. Bondy longest-cycle minimum-degree conjecture

**Statement.** Let $k\ge1$ and let $G$ be a finite simple $k$-vertex-connected graph on $n$ vertices with $(k+1)\delta(G)\ge n+k(k-1)$. For every longest cycle $C$, each path in the subgraph induced by vertices outside $C$ has at most $k-1$ vertices. Longest cycles under this density condition should leave no long connected path behind. This is the minimum-degree specialization of Bondy’s 1980 conjecture about degree sums of independent sets. [Ma, Ning and Zhao, Conjecture 1 and introduction](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnag249).

**Frontier and significance.** The specialization extends the Dirac and Nash-Williams phenomena and translates local degree/connectivity assumptions into structure outside longest cycles. Ma, Ning and Zhao's paper, published October 26, 2026, records $k\le3$ as settled and proves its Theorem 1.1 for $n\ge5k^2+7k$. The exact $k=4$ gap is therefore confined below 108 vertices by that theorem. The overall residual question still has unbounded $k$. A November 22 search located no full resolution of this specialization. [Journal article and explicit bound](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/2026/21/rnag249/9002305).

**Lean.** `Arxiv.«2606.03696».bondy_conjecture` matches the specialization. The repository defines vertex connectivity by surviving deletions of fewer than $k$ vertices and longest by equality to the circumference. The off-cycle graph excludes walk support, and `P.support.length + 1 ≤ k` counts the path’s vertices. Mathlib supplies finite simple graphs, walks and induced subgraphs. Its project extensions supply vertex connectivity. The explicit large-order theorem is a statement with `sorry` in the same file, so a complete Lean result must account for that dependence. [Pinned declarations](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/2606.03696/BondyLongestCycles.lean#L60).

**Research plan.** The size cutoff suggests a sharpened finite reduction at $k=4$, combining the paper’s DFS and circumference techniques with exact obstruction search. A disproof needs a certified longest cycle and an outside path with at least four vertices. Use the classical circumference lower bound $\min(n,2\delta)$ as a sanity filter. For a counterexample at $k=4$, its longest cycle must have at most $n-4$ vertices. Start with selected orders 16–22. Fix a cycle length consistent with both bounds and a disjoint four-vertex path. Generate graphs near the degree threshold and use an exact cycle oracle to reject false candidates. Track which edge and attachment patterns force longer cycles. This is a bounded CPU study. Exhaustive graph generation near 107 vertices is unrealistic without strong structural reductions.

**Board assessment.** High-value. 75/100. Grades in model order (5, 3, 3, 4, 4, 3) out of five. A published finite range for each k focuses work. Even the first unresolved k is too large for naive exhaustion. Uncertainty: Moderate; scale of residual cases.

## 8. Černý quadratic reset-word bound

**Statement.** Every synchronizing complete deterministic automaton with $n\ge2$ states has a reset word of length at most $(n-1)^2$. A reset word takes every state to the same final state regardless of where it starts. The bound is attained by Černý’s examples. Establishing it would replace the cubic general bound with a sharp quadratic one. [Černý, Pirická and Rosenauerová, 1971](https://www.kybernetika.cz/content/1971/4/289) · [Szykuła’s November 18, 2026 survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24245).

**Frontier.** Open in general as surveyed November 18, 2026. Shitov’s 2019 result is an established cubic upper bound. A November 2026 preprint claims a smaller cubic coefficient and declares no human verification. Zhu’s July 2026 preprint supplies an affirmative result for one-cluster automata of positive level. Goel’s November 19 preprint reports a proof for quasi-Eulerian degree one and covers 46 of 49 binary strongly connected configurations at degree two. It links Lean proof artifacts. These scoped results show active competition. Their Lean replay was outside this status review. [Volkov’s results list, January 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15655) · [November cubic claim](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00172v1) · [Zhu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19675) · [Goel and scope](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00635v1) · [Goel’s Lean repository](https://github.com/david44hello/paper_cerny-1969-conjecture-quasi-eulerian/tree/v4.50).

**Lean.** `CernyConjecture.cerny_conjecture` uses Mathlib’s `DFA`, finite states and `List` words. The reset predicate quantifies over every starting state and is independent of acceptance states. It handles the singleton case and admits arbitrary letter types. The finite-alphabet convention can be recovered using the finite induced transition maps. Finite-word witnesses can be checked cheaply. General linear-algebra arguments and any certificate of shortest-word optimality need additional work. [Pinned declaration](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/Wikipedia/CernyConjecture.lean#L60).

**Research plan.** The central obstacle is bounding the cumulative cost of compressing successive image sets. First reproduce the classic quadratic examples with breadth-first search over subset masks. Then sample synchronizing binary automata across the three degree-two configurations outside Goel’s stated coverage, using that paper’s exact hypotheses, and profile shortest words that expand preimages or compress images. Clamp initial sizes to about 8–14 states for a CPU pilot. Look for a reusable structural inequality. A counterexample would need both a reset witness and certified absence of reset words within the bound. Generic sampling tends to produce easy automata and the subset search has exponential worst-case growth.

**Board assessment.** Moonshot. 74/100. Grades in model order (5, 2, 5, 4, 2, 4) out of five. Sharp first-party benchmark with a scoped theory frontier. A general reset-word proof is a major leap. Uncertainty: High confidence in documented general frontier; low tractability.

## 9. Green 40 binary linear radius-two covering

**Canonical ID and exact statement.** `Green40.green_40.f_two_eq_one`, the fixed-radius question accompanying Ben Green’s Problem 40. For $C\le\mathbb F_2^n$ and $H_2(n)=\{x:\mathrm{wt}(x)\le2\}$, write $d(n)$ for the minimum of $|C||H_2(n)|/2^n$ over linear subspaces with $C+H_2(n)=\mathbb F_2^n$. Is $\liminf_{n\to\infty}d(n)=1$? This asks if radius-two balls about a binary linear code can cover with asymptotically vanishing fractional excess along some unbounded sequence of lengths. It would pinpoint where sphere-covering efficiency meets the linearity constraint. [Green, Problem 40](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf#problem.40)

**Status and partial results.** The sphere-covering bound gives $f(2)\ge1$. Davydov, Marcugini, Pambianco and Wu’s Oct. 11, 2026 v2 constructs an infinite family and gives $f(2)\le2601/2048<1.27002$ using $q^m$-concatenation and a 50-column seed of redundancy 10. This supersedes the 1.4238 baseline cited in Lean. The Nov. 22 status check found no closure of the remaining gap. Nonlinear covers and the separate radius-one equality do not address the linear radius-two limit. The recent construction makes this an active specialty. [Davydov et al., Theorem 2.1 and §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16078v2)

**Strategy and first experiment.** At the seed level, check whether every binary syndrome of dimension 10 is zero, a column or an XOR of two columns. Reproduce the paper’s seed and partition, then inspect the existing 49-column near-misses and change the search model before revisiting that size. Wu reports a seven-syndrome deficit, no repair by exchanging up to five columns around one near-miss, and inconclusive exact runs. Compare a symmetry-reduced partition-aware model against that baseline. Record exact XOR coverage, partition properties and search limits. CPU feasibility for the checker is high and for the search uncertain. An improved seed may suggest a better bound if the lifting theorem applies. Equality needs families approaching density 1 or, for disproof, a uniform positive lower gap. A dense search over single seeds or densities converging to a larger constant leaves the exact target open. [Wu’s search notes and replay links](https://github.com/wustep/maths/blob/main/problems/covering/explainer.html)

**Lean.** The pinned file defines a Hamming ball, covering subspace, normalized cardinality and the `liminf` in `ENNReal`, matching the intended asymptotic subsequence formulation. Mathlib’s `ZMod`, `Submodule`, finite cardinality and filter APIs support an exact seed checker. A full certificate would need the parity-check covering bridge and substantial family or asymptotic infrastructure. [Pinned Lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2207c54d0dbe960de591d280f997b195bf3f6e28/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/40.lean)

**Board assessment.** Moonshot. 73/100. Grades in model order (4, 3, 4, 4, 4, 3) out of five. Recent construction gives a concrete seed. A global density-one family or lower bound needs a much larger conceptual step. Uncertainty: Moderate; finite seed to asymptotic gap.

## 10. Uniqueness of crystal components

**Canonical ID and exact statement.** Abrate–Barbero–Cerruti–Murru, *The Biharmonic Mean*, §4, `Arxiv.«1601.03081».crystals_components_unique`. Put $B(a,b)=((a+b)^2+(ab+1)^2)/(2(a+1)(b+1))$. For odd $N=ab=cd$ with all factors greater than 1, if $B(a,b)$ and $B(c,d)$ are integers, then $\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}$. These admissible factorizations define crystals. The question asks whether the multiplicative output of the paper’s recurrence construction remembers the inputs. Its significance lies in the arithmetic of recurrence collisions and divisibility. [Original 2016 paper, pp. 490–495](https://imar.ro/journals/Mathematical_Reports/Pdfs/2016/4/5.pdf)

**Status and partial results.** The original paper leaves uniqueness open. Proposition 1 rewrites the condition as integrality of $(a+b)^2/((a+1)(b+1))=w$. Theorems 4–5 describe admissible factor pairs, up to order, by successive values of a recurrence at parameter $w$ after the change of variables $(a,b)=(2u-1,2v-1)$. This classifies the possible pairs and leaves collisions between their products unresolved. Searches on Nov. 22, 2026 found the 2016 question and its Lean formalization, with no newer primary resolution located. Low visible competition accompanies weaker evidence about unpublished work and more specialized significance.

**Strategy and first experiment.** Use independent exact routines to enumerate divisibility-qualified unordered factor pairs with product at most $10^6$ and to generate recurrence pairs on the same domain. Compare pair sets and record products by recurrence parameter and index. Calibrate with the published formal example $35=5\cdot7$. Use the first run to verify generator coverage and isolate the smallest plausible cross-parameter collisions or recurring modular obstructions. A genuine collision gives a small direct witness. A proof needs a global collision exclusion, potentially from recovering the parameter or index from the product. One CPU day is reasonable for the pilot. Beware incomplete recurrence cutoffs, even factors, overflow and confusing unrelated biharmonic-number tables with crystal data.
