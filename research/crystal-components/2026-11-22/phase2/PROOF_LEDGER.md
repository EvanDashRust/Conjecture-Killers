## Rejected proof routes and remaining claim
1. Removing the individual successors permits the infinite orbit. A common divisor of M is a necessary projected test. Reconstruction also needs both linear inverse equations, properness, parity, product and both unprojected successor tests.
2. A primitive-conic Vieta jump would require a signed integer z with (ur)² A z+c²=(ur)² B². This forces ur to divide c² despite their coprimality and ur>1. The formula includes zero and negative z. It gives no descent. Subtracting k and l separately produces different products k²−1 and l²−1. The matched half-shifts give new identities but supply no smaller qualifying collision.
3. A least nontrivial idempotent smaller than the half-successor can occur in a real crystal. The example n=527, L=5, z=3, w=10 satisfies all one-pair conditions, so the next argument must use the competing pair.
4. The next target is the unit-axis exclusion for prime-power R with a composite higher half-successor. In the smallest open support for (n+1)/2, namely three primes, its larger half-successor must use exactly two of them and the odd higher quotient is a power of the third. For a two-prime half-successor there are exactly two nontrivial idempotent residues. Intersect those with the two matched shifts, exact reconstruction and both successor tests. These requirements remain unbounded. General four-cell exchanges still require a separate argument.
