## Remaining obstruction and next proof target

The universally quantified one-index claim remains open. For exactly three full prime-power blocks sorted R≤P≤Q, the smallest singleton R fails the size rule. Two surviving splits would be (P,RQ) and (Q,RP). The exchange obstructions cover R=3 and R=5. A general cross-parameter recurrence collision also remains possible outside the proved classes. Increasing an index or parameter along a single recurrence does not exclude it. For w=3 the sequence starts 0,1,3,4,3,1,0, so indices 3 and 4 give the same unordered pair. The formal descent starts with x=1 and uses only positive solutions. It never uses the printed equality 2u₀−1=0, which should be −1.

A further hand derivation explains why the factor-3 argument does not simply extend to 5. For odd R and distinct coprime blocks P,Q,R, set S=P+RQ, T=Q+RP and g=gcd(S,T). Both sums divide n+1. Since lcm(S,T)≤n+1 and ST>R(n+1), one has g>R. From RT−S=(R²−1)P and gcd(g,P)=1 one gets g dividing R²−1. Writing S=gA and T=gB gives coprime A,B. The odd quotients force A,B to have the same parity, hence both are odd. Therefore (RT−S)/g is even, and P odd implies 2g divides R²−1. For R=5, g=12 is still permitted. This is a necessary arithmetic filter only, and this general-R argument was not compiled.

The remaining R=5 case was then closed. Since ST≥36PQ and PQ≥9, ST>7(n+1). Writing n+1=m·ST/g forces m=1 when g=12. The corresponding quadratic and finite certificate are recorded above. An exploratory single-process Python evaluation examined the 32 odd values d−1 with d an even divisor of 3024, hence 1,024 ordered pairs. Its only quadratic solutions were (1,3) and (3,1), which violate k≥3 on one side. Shell invocation wall time was 2.38 seconds including overhead; its CPU was not separately metered. Five.lean supplies an independent kernel certificate for *all* candidates ≥3 dividing through their successors, with no oddness assumption needed at that stage.

For arbitrary coprime repartitions, grouping the four intersections of the prime-power supports yields the form (ux,ry),(uy,rx). Distinct unordered pairs allow orientation u<r: the intersection blocks u and r are coprime, and u=r forces both to be 1 and the pairs to be swaps. This grouping follows from unique prime factorization and is given here on paper. The verified height theorem itself accepts the displayed factorizations directly. The quotient divisibilities k+1∣Cl and l+1∣Ck make the fixed-block bound effective. Its proof sets Cl=(k+1)v, Ck=(l+1)w, whence (C²−vw)k=vw+Cw. Thus vw<C², w≤C² and k≤C²+C³. With a,b the components of the first pair, (a−k)(b−k)=k²−1, giving the stated bound on both components and n.

The next proof target is a descent from a least collision to a smaller collision or a uniform exclusion of mutually divisible quotients when the exchanged blocks vary. The fixed-block height bound alone permits unbounded u,r and unbounded n. Testing progressively larger R would attack separate finite subproblems. This checkpoint stops at the structural obstruction, without initiating such an expansion.
